Remote Online Notarization

What is a Remote Online Notary?

Online notarization combines the traditional role of a notary with secure electronic technology.

Notarization without an in-person meeting

Remote Online Notarization, commonly called RON, allows a signer and an online notary to communicate in real time through audio-video technology while completing an eligible electronic notarization.

Instead of driving to a notary office, the signer participates from a compatible computer, tablet, or other supported device.

A Florida notarial act performed online

Sean M. Keyser Notary Solutions provides Remote Online Notary services under Florida law while Sean is physically located in Florida.

The signer may be located elsewhere, which makes RON especially useful when distance, travel, scheduling, or mobility makes an in-person appointment inconvenient.

Is RON Right for Your Document?

A quick check before scheduling.

Remote Online Notarization can be extremely convenient, but it isn't the right method for every transaction.

RON May Be a Good Fit When

  • You cannot conveniently meet a notary in person
  • You are traveling or located outside Florida
  • Multiple participants are in different locations
  • You need an eligible document handled electronically
  • The receiving organization accepts Remote Online Notarization

Confirm Before Proceeding When

  • The receiving party has special notarization instructions
  • The document will be recorded or filed with an agency
  • A title company, lender, court, attorney, or financial institution provided the document
  • You are unsure whether an electronically notarized document will be accepted
  • The document has special witness or execution requirements

Remote Online Notary FAQ

Frequently asked questions about online notarization.

Can I use Remote Online Notarization if I am not in Florida?

Yes. A Florida online notary who is physically located in Florida may perform a compliant online notarization when the principal or witnesses are outside Florida, subject to the document and transaction requirements.

How much does Remote Online Notarization cost?

The fee is $25 for the first notarial act and $15 for each additional notarial act completed during the same online session. If a witness is provided by the notary, an additional $25 fee applies.

What do I need for a Remote Online Notary appointment?

You generally need reliable internet access, a compatible device with a camera and microphone, an acceptable government-issued identification credential, your document, and any required additional signers or witnesses.

Can every document be notarized online?

No. Whether RON is appropriate can depend on the document, applicable requirements, and whether the receiving organization accepts an electronically notarized document.

How is my identity verified online?

When a remotely appearing principal is not personally known to the online notary, applicable Florida RON procedures include remote presentation of identification, credential analysis, and identity proofing.

Is Remote Online Notarization just a video call with a notary?

No. A compliant Florida online notarization involves more than an ordinary video meeting. The transaction uses required audio-video communication and online-notarization procedures for identity verification, electronic execution, and recordkeeping.

Can witnesses participate remotely?

Florida law provides procedures for online notarizations involving remotely participating witnesses in certain circumstances. Whether remote witnessing is appropriate depends on the document and transaction requirements.

What if the organization receiving my document does not accept RON?

You should follow the receiving organization's requirements. If an in-person notarization is required and you are located in Lake County, Florida, you can also review Lake County mobile notary service.

Can you tell me which notarial certificate or document I need?

I can explain the notarial process, but I cannot choose a legal document or notarial act for you when doing so would constitute legal advice. If you are unsure, contact the document preparer, receiving organization, or a qualified attorney for guidance.
