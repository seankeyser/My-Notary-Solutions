Remote Online Notary Guide

What Documents Can Be Notarized Online?

Many documents that require a traditional acknowledgment, oath, affirmation, or other permitted notarial act may also be eligible for Remote Online Notarization (RON). Florida law allows a registered online notary to perform notarial functions authorized under Florida's general notary laws through a compliant online process, with an important exception for solemnizing marriage.

That does not mean every document should automatically be handled online. The document's requirements, witness requirements, electronic-signature format, and the policies of the organization that will receive or record the document can all affect whether RON is appropriate.

Quick Answer

Common documents may qualify for online notarization.

Affidavits, sworn statements, powers of attorney, contracts, business documents, financial forms, certain real-estate documents, and other electronic records may be candidates for RON when the required notarial act can be completed online and the receiving party accepts the transaction.

1. Affidavits and Sworn Statements

Affidavits and sworn statements are common candidates for online notarization when the document requires a jurat or another oath or affirmation. During the RON session, the signer appears before the online notary through live audio-video communication and completes the required notarial procedure.

The document should already contain the appropriate notarial certificate or the signer should receive direction from the document's issuing or receiving organization about the type of notarial act required. A notary cannot choose a legal notarial certificate for a signer when doing so would amount to legal advice.

2. Powers of Attorney

A power of attorney may be capable of online notarization, but this category deserves extra attention. Some powers of attorney have witness requirements or grant authorities that trigger additional Florida procedures when witnesses participate remotely.

If your power of attorney was prepared by an attorney, financial institution, title company, or another professional, confirm that the document is intended for electronic execution and Remote Online Notarization before scheduling.

Important: Florida law contains additional remote-witnessing procedures for certain powers of attorney and other sensitive estate or health-related records. These documents should be reviewed for their execution requirements before the RON appointment.

3. Contracts and Business Documents

Contracts, agreements, corporate forms, business authorizations, and other commercial documents may qualify when notarization is required and the parties permit electronic execution. RON can be especially useful when business participants are located in different cities or states.

Examples can include agreements, corporate acknowledgments, sworn business statements, and other records that contain an appropriate notarial certificate.

4. Banking, Insurance, and Financial Documents

Certain banking, insurance, and financial documents may be appropriate for online notarization. However, banks, insurers, investment companies, and other financial institutions can have their own acceptance standards, so it is wise to confirm RON acceptance before the session.

For powers of attorney involving financial authority, additional legal and witnessing requirements may apply depending on the powers granted by the document.

5. Real-Estate and Mortgage Documents

Some real-estate and mortgage documents can be completed using Remote Online Notarization when the transaction participants, title or settlement company, lender, and recording requirements allow it.

Because real-estate transactions often involve multiple documents and third-party requirements, confirm with the title company, lender, settlement agent, attorney, or recording authority before relying on RON.

6. Medical and Healthcare Forms

Certain medical or healthcare-related documents that require notarization may qualify for RON. Health care advance directives and other sensitive records can involve special witnessing procedures, particularly when witnesses are participating remotely.

For these documents, confirm the required signing and witnessing process with the organization or legal professional that prepared or will receive the document.

7. Trust, Estate, and Electronic Will Documents

Florida law provides procedures for electronic wills and other estate-planning records, but these are not ordinary RON transactions. Electronic wills can involve witnesses, specific audio-video procedures, and qualified-custodian requirements.

Wills, revocable trusts with testamentary aspects, certain powers of attorney, health care advance directives, and similar records may also trigger additional remote-witnessing protections under Florida law. For that reason, these documents should not be treated as a routine "upload and notarize" appointment.

Special-Handling Documents

Some documents need more than a standard online notary session.

Estate-planning, healthcare, financial-authority, and witness-dependent documents may have additional execution requirements. The safest approach is to confirm the document's requirements with the attorney, institution, title company, or other receiving party before the appointment.

What Is Not a Remote Online Notarization?

I-9 Authorized Representative Service

Completing Form I-9 as an employer's authorized representative is not a notarial act. No notary seal is applied merely because a notary serves as the employer's designated representative.

Marriage Ceremonies

Florida's online-notary statute does not authorize solemnizing a marriage as an online notarization. Marriage solemnization is excluded from the functions that a Florida online notary may perform through RON.

Services Requiring Physical Inspection

A service that depends on physically inspecting property, an object, or an original item may not be replaceable by a RON session merely because some related paperwork requires a signature. For example, a separate verification service can involve requirements beyond notarizing an electronic signature.

The Receiving Party Still Matters

A document may be legally capable of electronic notarization while a particular lender, title company, court, government agency, financial institution, foreign authority, or other recipient has additional format or acceptance requirements.

Before scheduling RON for an important transaction, ask the receiving party whether it accepts an electronically signed and remotely notarized document.

Can I Use a Florida Online Notary If I Am Outside Florida?

Yes, the signer does not generally have to be physically located in Florida. A Florida online notary who is physically located in Florida may perform a compliant online notarization even when the principal or witnesses are elsewhere, subject to the applicable document and transaction requirements.

When the principal is outside Florida, Florida law requires confirmation that the principal wants the notarial act performed by a Florida notary under Florida law.

How Do I Know Whether My Document Qualifies?

Before requesting an online appointment, check four things:

  • Notarial certificate: Does the document indicate the notarial act that is required?
  • Electronic format: Can the document be signed and completed electronically?
  • Witnesses: Are witnesses required, and are there special rules for those witnesses?
  • Receiving party: Will the organization receiving the document accept RON?

Florida RON Law References

For readers who want to review the governing rules directly, Florida's online-notary provisions are found in Part II of Chapter 117, Florida Statutes. The sections most relevant to this guide include authority to perform online notarizations, online procedures, and remote witnessing.

  • Florida Statute 117.209 — Authority to perform online notarizations
  • Florida Statute 117.265 — Online notarization procedures
  • Florida Statute 117.285 — Supervising the witnessing of electronic records
